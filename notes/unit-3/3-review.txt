o Imperial expansion
-> Gunpowder cannons breached walls; firearms aided conquest.
-> Funding, supply lines, and alliances also enabled conquest.
-> Ottomans expanded in southeastern Europe and the Middle East.
-> Ottoman cannons helped take Constantinople in 1453.
-> Safavids consolidated Persia under Shah Ismail.
-> Babur came from Central Asia; guns aided conquest in India.
-> Manchus took Beijing in 1644; Qing later subdued Mongolia.
-> Armed fur trade helped Russia's Cossacks conquer Siberia.
o Military and bureaucratic rule
-> Ottoman devshirme forcibly recruited Christian boys.
-> Converts trained as Janissaries or imperial officials.
-> Janissaries served as professional infantry for the sultan.
-> Qing preserved Confucian exams and scholar-official rule.
-> Unlike Ottoman devshirme, Qing exams tested elite learning.
o Taxation and imperial revenue
-> Ottoman tax farmers paid for rights to collect taxes.
-> Mughal zamindars collected land revenue from peasants.
-> Both used intermediaries; local power limited central rule.
-> Taxes financed troops, enabling further imperial expansion.

o Religious change in Europe
-> Luther's Reformation challenged Catholic authority in 1517.
-> Printing spread Protestant ideas; some princes backed reform.
-> Catholic reform: Trent reaffirmed doctrine, curbed abuses.
-> Counter-Reformation Jesuits expanded schools and missions.
-> Confessional divisions fueled wars and religious persecution.
o Religion and imperial diversity
-> Ottoman sultans upheld Sunni Islam to legitimize rule.
-> Safavid shahs made Twelver Shi'a Islam a state religion.
-> Their wars over land and power sharpened Sunni-Shi'a divides.
-> Akbar's Hindu alliances and tolerance bolstered Mughal rule.
-> Unlike Akbar, Aurangzeb restored jizya on non-Muslims.
-> Sikhism arose in Punjab amid Hindu-Muslim interaction.
-> Guru Nanak taught one God; Sikhs formed a distinct tradition.
o Legitimizing imperial authority
-> Louis XIV claimed divine right; French nobles kept influence.
-> Versailles displayed royal power and drew nobles to court.
-> Shah Jahan's Taj Mahal displayed Mughal wealth and piety.
-> French and Mughal monuments both exalted ruling dynasties.
-> Qing retained the Mandate of Heaven to justify dynastic rule.
