o Expansion and military organization
-> Both Ottomans and Mughals used cannon-equipped armies.
-> Janissaries drew pay; mansabdars often held revenue grants.
-> Janissaries were infantry; mansabdars mustered cavalry.
-> Qing banners organized Manchu, Mongol, and Han soldiers.
-> Qing banners were hereditary; devshirme recruited outsiders.
-> Ottoman and Mughal firearms needed taxes and local allies.
-> Cavalry persisted in Ottoman and Mughal armies.
o Geography and imperial motives
-> Russia sought Siberian furs; Qing sought frontier security.
-> Both used forts to secure frontiers and trade.
-> Siberia's rivers aided Cossacks; Qing crossed steppe routes.
-> Unlike Russia's fur tribute, Mughal wealth relied on farms.
-> Agrarian density made land taxes lucrative for Mughals.
o Bureaucratic recruitment
-> Ottomans coerced devshirme boys; Qing used Confucian exams.
-> Both trained officials to serve imperial courts.
-> Qing exams favored gentry; devshirme bypassed birth elites.
-> Qing kept exams to enlist Chinese elite cooperation.
-> Ottoman converts' dependence aimed to bind them to sultans.

o Revenue and local elites
-> Ottoman tax farmers and Mughal zamindars collected revenue.
-> Both linked imperial demands to local rural communities.
-> Tax farmers bought rights; zamindars held local land claims.
-> Both funded armies, but local collectors retained leverage.
-> Distance made both courts depend on intermediaries.
o Religion and monumental authority
-> Louis XIV claimed divine right; Qing used Heaven's mandate.
-> Both justified kingship through a sacred political order.
-> French Versailles and Mughal Taj Mahal exalted dynasties.
-> Versailles hosted nobles; the Taj Mahal was a royal tomb.
-> Both used patronage, but buildings served different roles.
-> Such displays reinforced claims, not total central control.
o Governing imperial diversity
-> Ottomans were Sunni; Safavids promoted Twelver Shi'a.
-> Both used Islam; rival identities sharpened state conflict.
-> Ottomans kept jizya; Akbar abolished it in Mughal lands.
-> Akbar recruited Hindus; Qing retained Han scholar-officials.
-> Both co-opted subject elites rather than erasing difference.
-> Mughal and Qing rulers mixed accommodation with coercion.
