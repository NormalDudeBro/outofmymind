o Fragmentation and legitimacy
-> By 1200, Abbasid political control was largely in Iraq.
-> Regional dynasties replaced centralized caliphal rule.
-> Turkic migration supplied soldiers and new ruling elites.
-> Seljuk Turks kept caliphs as symbols of legitimacy.
-> Mongols sacked Baghdad in 1258, ending its caliphate.
o Regional state formation
-> Muslim rulers used armies, taxation, and Islamic law.
-> Mamluks: military slaves seized Egypt's throne in 1250.
-> Mamluks held Egypt and Syria, checking Mongol expansion.
-> Delhi Sultanate formed in 1206 through Turkic conquest.
-> Delhi used Persian administration and local Hindu elites.
-> Ottomans emerged c. 1299, expanding from Anatolia.
o Trade and social structures
-> Trade linked cities across Dar al-Islam.
-> Muslim merchants connected land and Indian Ocean routes.
-> Urban markets and craft production enriched ruling elites.
-> Land taxes and irrigation supported agrarian economies.
-> Social rank reflected wealth, lineage, and slave status.
-> Women held property rights but faced patriarchal limits.

o Religious communities
-> Islam linked diverse regions through belief and law.
-> Ulama interpreted Islamic law; madrasas trained scholars.
-> Sufi teachers spread Islam through local networks.
-> Judaism and Christianity continued under Muslim rule.
-> Jews and Christians often paid jizya for protected status.
-> Legal protection did not mean social or religious equality.
o Intellectual innovations
-> Scholars advanced algebra, astronomy, and medicine.
-> Hospitals and medical texts supported clinical learning.
-> Nasir al-Din al-Tusi improved astronomical models.
-> Ibn Rushd's works shaped later philosophical debate.
-> Intellectual work built on Greek, Persian, Indian learning.
o Knowledge exchange
-> Arabic connected scholars across Muslim societies.
-> Translations preserved and expanded older knowledge.
-> Paper from China helped books and scholarship spread.
-> Trade and travel carried texts and technologies.
-> Al-Andalus and Sicily linked Islamic and European learning.
-> Knowledge exchange influenced European universities.
