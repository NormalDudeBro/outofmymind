o Regional environments
-> In c. 1200-1450, Africa had diverse forms of government.
-> Sahel grasslands linked farming zones to Saharan routes.
-> Camel caravans crossed deserts, connecting gold and salt.
-> Trade taxes and farm surpluses supported state growth.
o Mali and gold trade
-> Sundiata founded Mali in the 1200s through conquest.
-> Rulers taxed gold and salt trade across the Sahara.
-> Provincial leaders governed under Mali's king.
-> Niger River farming sustained towns and royal revenues.
o Islam and royal authority
-> Mali's rulers adopted Islam, joining Muslim trade networks.
-> Mansa Musa's 1324-1325 hajj displayed Mali's wealth.
-> Royal patronage supported mosques and learning in Timbuktu.
-> Many rural communities retained local religious practices.
o Great Zimbabwe
-> Great Zimbabwe grew powerful in southern Africa by c. 1300.
-> Cattle and farming supported rulers and social hierarchies.
-> Gold exports reached Indian Ocean ports through middlemen.

o Swahili city states
-> Kilwa and Mombasa were independent coastal trading cities.
-> Monsoon winds linked East Africa to Arabia and India.
-> Merchants exported gold, ivory, and enslaved people.
-> Port wealth supported ruling elites and urban growth.
-> Islam spread through trade; many coastal elites converted.
-> Swahili joined Bantu grammar with Arabic loanwords.
o Ethiopia and Christianity
-> The Solomonic dynasty took power in Ethiopia in 1270.
-> Claims of descent from Solomon legitimized kingship.
-> Highland farming sustained landholding nobles and rulers.
-> Christianity tied royal legitimacy to church institutions.
-> Rock-cut churches at Lalibela expressed Christian devotion.
-> Ethiopia retained Christianity amid nearby Muslim states.
o Diverse African societies
-> Mali was an empire; Swahili ports were separate city states.
-> Zimbabwe's stone walls marked elite wealth and power.
-> Ethiopia's Christian kings differed from Muslim Mali rulers.
-> Kinship councils governed many societies without kings.
-> Trade enriched elites; local beliefs and kinship persisted.
